About the Author

Anni Albers (1899–1994) was a German-born textile artist and educator who helped push weaving into the modern art world. She trained at the Bauhaus, where she became known for treating textiles like a serious design problem instead of “just craft.” Her work mixes structure, material, and clean abstraction, and it ended up shaping a lot of 20th-century design thinking. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

In 1933, she and Josef Albers left Germany and moved to the U.S., where she taught and worked at Black Mountain College. Later, she wrote On Weaving (first published in 1965), which became one of the most important books ever written about textiles, not as decoration, but as design and visual organization. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}
